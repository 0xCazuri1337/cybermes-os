## VERDICT

**No — this corpus is not trustworthy as evidence today, and the reason is not the one
the metrics are designed to detect.** The pipeline's own machinery is genuinely sound:
`selftest` passes end to end, `verify_quotes` confirms 70/70 quotes across the four
classes I ran it on, sources parity holds at 167 URLs with a closed role vocabulary,
and every `skill_path`/`methodology` field resolves to a file that exists on disk. The
fabrication failure mode the quality gate was written to stop — invented citations, dead
links, single-publisher stubs — has been successfully eliminated. But the gate
(`wks/quality.py::assess`) measures **how many publishers an item cites, never whether
those publishers' quotes support the item's claim.** That single omission is
load-bearing: I re-ran `assess()` over all 1,111 active items and it returns
`high_confidence` 1,111 times with **zero** hold reasons, including for items whose
supporting quote is a HackTheBox course-marketing sentence that has nothing to do with
the claim it is attached to. Layered on top, 355 items (31.95%) are exact-duplicate
claim text spread across up to 11 unrelated classes, and that duplication is what
carries **32 of 62 classes** over the 12-item maturity bar. So the corpus's headline
numbers — 100% promotion, 100% disproof coverage, 100% class maturity, 0% unsupported
claims, 10.08% duplication — are all *arithmetically true and substantively misleading*.
Read this store as a well-cited list of hyperlinks; do not read it as evidence that any
given claim is true until someone checks the quote against the claim by hand.

## WHAT IS SOUND

These were run and **passed**. A reader should know what was actually exercised.

- **Full gate self-test passes.**
  `python -m wks.cli selftest` → final line **`ALL WKS GATE TESTS PASS`**, including named anti-regression cases: *"dry run wrote nothing to the canonical db — before=0 after=0"*, *"two hosts of ONE publisher are refused — state=under_review reasons=['INSUFFICIENT_PUBLISHER_HOSTS(1<2:learn.microsoft.com,www.microsoft.com)']"*, *"builder-only item is not promoted — under_review"*, *"a quote no revision contains is still refused"*, *"a dead url with no successor is not rewritten (laundering guard)"*, *"pinned tags are searched newest first"*.
- **Quotes are really on the pages — I re-fetched and checked myself, independently of the store's own verifier.** This is the biggest positive result and it rules out fabrication, which is what the corpus was rebuilt to eliminate.
  - `python -m wks.verify_quotes --class csrf` → `VERIFIED=19`
  - `python -m wks.verify_quotes --class ad_ldap` → `VERIFIED=21`
  - `python -m wks.verify_quotes --class misconfig` → `VERIFIED=13`
  - `python -m wks.verify_quotes --class smuggling` → `VERIFIED=17`
  - My own strict checker (normalised **substring only**, no loose variants, `wks/verify_quotes.py`'s five-variant fallback not used), 24 HTB citations → **`strict_hit=21/24  fetch_failed=0  strict_MISS=3`**; 40 mixed citations → **`strict_hit=33/40  fetch_failed=7  strict_MISS=0`** (the 7 were W3C HTTP 403, i.e. blocked, not wrong). Where I printed page context, e.g. `ad_delegation.prerequisite.delegation-abuse-requires-in-forest-delegation-config`, the quote `dispelling the notion that the forest is the security boundary.` was present on the fetched page.
  - The three strict misses are **not** fabrications either — manual context dump shows them on-page with markup-normalisation differences (e.g. `race.tech.parallel_burst_thread_count` → `STRICT SUBSTRING PRESENT: True`; `authn.auto.03` quote found as `by setting the loggedin cookie to true , the attacker bypasses…`, the stored quote using straight quotes).
- **Source registry is internally consistent and parity-checked.**
  `python tools/check_sources_parity.py` → `registry url-slots=198 distinct=167 merged=167` / `PARITY OK — no URL lost, none invented, role separation held, vocabulary closed.`
  `python tools/build_sources.py --check` → `PARITY OK — 167 sources (normative=140 lead=27) across 20 hosts / 16 publisher orgs, 62 classes`.
- **Taxonomy write-side is consistent.** `python tools/taxonomy/taxonomy_write.py --check` → `OK — 142 files match the spec and the canonical db`. (The *read*-side auditor, `audit_taxonomy.py`, does **not** pass — see defect 9.)
- **Source-intake eval passes, including negative controls.**
  `python eval/test_wks_source_intake.py` → `ALL PASS`, with `T25.4 a 200 that is a JS shell is refused (negative control) PASS`, `keyword-stuffed shell refused`, `T25.4b a long real article with a JS footer is KEPT PASS`, `an already-registered URL cannot enter twice PASS`.
- **The hard publisher gate is real and does bind.** I recomputed it independently over all 1,111 active items: **0 items fail `MIN_PUBLISHER_HOSTS>=2`** (1050 items cite exactly 2 orgs, 59 cite 3, 2 cite 4). Two pages of one company genuinely cannot corroborate each other — `wks/quality.py:102` collapses subdomains via `config.publisher_org`. Confirmed by the selftest case cited above.
- **Builder pages are correctly excluded from the publisher count.** Selftest asserts `is_builder_host()` for `app.notion.com`, `*.notion.site`, `*.gitbook.io`, `*.readthedocs.io`, `*.netlify.app`, and that a builder-only item lands in `under_review` with `INSUFFICIENT_PUBLISHER_HOSTS(0<2:)`.

## GAPS I COULD NOT MEASURE

Stated plainly rather than guessed.

- **Full-corpus quote verification.** `python -m wks.verify_quotes` over all 1,111 items ran past 680s twice and I killed it. I verified 70 items via the store's own verifier (4 classes, all `VERIFIED`) and 64 citations with my own stricter checker. **I did not verify the remaining ~1,000 items' quotes.** A small unknown number may be genuinely fabricated.
- **True quote→claim entailment.** I proved the gate *does not check* it and showed named counter-examples. I did **not** build or run a per-item entailment test across the corpus, so I cannot say how many of the other ~1,000 items also carry a non-supporting quote. The 197/289 HTB figure and the 1,019/1,111 zero-or-weak-overlap figure are **word-overlap proxies of my own design**, not entailment judgements — word overlap is necessary but not sufficient for support, so these numbers bound the problem loosely, not tightly.
- **`audit_taxonomy.py` findings I did not adjudicate.** I counted 262 findings and confirmed one class-home path is genuinely missing, but I did not decide whether `SECTION DRIFT` (91 files) or `CHAIN SCHEMA DRIFT` (71 files) reflects real knowledge gaps or a stale auditor schema. The `CLASS_HOME` finding is a **path disagreement** between writer and auditor (`knowledge/CLASS.md` exists, `<class>.md` does not), so it may be a tooling bug rather than a content gap — I flag it as a failing gate either way.
- **Whether the concurrent writer is still mutating the db.** `open_conflicts` went 65 → 0 and `wks.db` mtime advanced to 20:39:09 mid-audit. I do not know what changed the db, whether anything else did, or whether the corpus I snapshotted (sha256 `819ace…`) is still current. **Any figure here that is corpus-state-dependent should be re-taken against a fresh frozen copy before being acted on.**
- **Chain *correctness* (as opposed to structure).** I verified chains have 2–5 steps, span classes, and bind to items. I did **not** verify that any chain's steps are a valid attack progression — `chain_step.why` ("why it works") was not audited for accuracy, and 69 steps have no `item_id` at all to check against.
- **Real-world demonstration.** `real_world_demo` is computed purely from `source_type` membership (`quality.py:167`). I did not test whether any cited source actually documents exploitation of the claim it backs.
- **Retrieval relevance beyond 8 probes.** `python -m wks.cli probe` covers 8 fixed queries. `race` fails as `wrong_class`. I did not measure ranking quality generally, so one `wrong_class` is a floor on known retrieval defects, not a count.
- **Content correctness of the 501 authored (non-`auto`) items.** 612 items carry `.auto.` in their `item_key` and 499 do not; I audited the `.auto.` population for boilerplate and duplication. Whether the 499 authored claims are *true* is unmeasured — nothing in this corpus can establish that, and I did not attempt it.
