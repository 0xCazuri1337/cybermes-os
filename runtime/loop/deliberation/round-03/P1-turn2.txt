After reading the debate, I find **P3 Claim 3** wrongly overstated. The claim that a blind traversal attempt can be reliably scored from the status line alone assumes universal server behavior (403 for out-of-root, 404 for in-root missing). In practice, servers vary widely: many return 403 for all restricted paths regardless of whether they escape the document root, some return 404 for all not-found paths, and others use different status codes entirely. Status codes are heuristic signals at best, not proof of CWE-22.

After reading their arguments, I'm less sure about the durability of **P3 Claim 3** itself—specifically the assumption that status-line differentials are dependable. Controlled experiments that consistently verify whether the resolved pathname exits the base directory and that the response body contains verifiably out-of-directory content would settle the question.
