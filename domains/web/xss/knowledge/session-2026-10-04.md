# Session debrief — 2026-10-04

What changed for `xss` in this learning session, and what produced it.

## New items admitted (2, both promoted, 2 independent publishers each)

### 1. `xss.lead.bbd_jorianwoltjer.com` — Blob-URL sandbox escape
Claim as filed: *"Public writeup evidence for xss: Blob-URL sandbox escape to
XSS."* Promoted at **0.77**, corroborated by an independent second publisher.

Mechanism, from the cited article: a Blob URL is same-origin, so content served
from one inherits the creating origin's privileges — which lets a page hand
attacker-controlled markup to the browser under a trusted origin, unsandboxed,
without a navigation to an attacker host.

**Hunt consequence.** Self-XSS and "the victim would have to paste this" get
dismissed. A Blob-URL or `srcdoc` sink reachable with attacker input is not
self-XSS — the origin is doing the trusting. The falsifier that matters is
whether the sink can be reached with a value the attacker controls.

### 2. `xss.lead.bbd_zere.es` — self-XSS to account takeover
Claim as filed: *"Public writeup evidence for xss: self-XSS to account
takeover."* Promoted at **0.82**.

Mechanism, from the cited article: a self-XSS becomes an account-takeover class
bug when the injected script acts on the *victim's own authenticated session* —
the classic vector being a URL fragment the application reflects into the page,
which the browser never sends to the server. Promoted at a higher score than the
first item because both publishers agree on the mechanism.

**Hunt consequence.** "Self-XSS, not a vulnerability" is the wrong dismissal
when the payload rides in a fragment or a reflected parameter and executes with
the victim's cookies. Check the sink's *context*, not who typed the string.

## Read these two as anchors, not as knowledge

Both were produced by the aggregator harvest (`bugbountydaily` RSS → linked
article), so their claims are topic labels. The mechanism, disproof clause and
both verbatim quotes live in the item bodies and `DIGEST.md`. Converting them to
mechanism-level claims is outstanding work — this class has 99 promoted items
and is the corpus's largest, so the marginal value here is low compared to the
classes still short of maturity.
